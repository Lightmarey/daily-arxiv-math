**原始英文摘要**

We prove a critical Morrey rigidity theorem for the five&#45;dimensional stationary Navier&#45;&#45;Stokes equations. More precisely, every smooth solution on $\mathbb R^5\setminus\{0\}$ satisfying 

$$\sup_{R>0}R^{-2}\int_{B_R}|u|^3\,dx<\infty$$

 is identically zero, up to an additive constant in the pressure. This replaces the pointwise Type&#45;I control in the known higher&#45;dimensional rigidity theory by a velocity&#45;only, scale&#45;invariant averaged condition that allows spatial concentration. The proof develops a weak head&#45;pressure mechanism that does not rely on pointwise pressure estimates or classical normal traces. We reconstruct a canonical pressure from the velocity, derive a renormalized inequality for the positive head pressure, and introduce two monotone radial fluxes. Annular energy estimates, suitable&#45;weak compactness, and blow&#45;up and blow&#45;down limits are then used to identify the endpoint fluxes and force rigidity. As an application, we obtain a removable&#45;singularity criterion in dimension five: if a suitable weak solution is smooth away from one point and either its scale&#45;invariant Dirichlet energy or its cubic velocity Morrey quantity remains bounded near that point, then the singularity is removable. Thus, within the isolated&#45;singularity class, the smallness assumption in the classical stationary regularity criterion is replaced by boundedness. We also prove the corresponding velocity&#45;only cubic Morrey rigidity theorem in dimension four by a different finite&#45;energy argument.
